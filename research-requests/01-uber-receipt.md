# Research 01, the Uber receipt

Feeds the `receipt-uber` slide in the open-weights deck. The slide's job:
"remember when an Uber cost $10 to <specific trip>?" — a memory the audience
actually has — then today's price for the same trip, then the dates that prove
the $10 era was the subsidy era. Spoken argument on this slide is price
elasticity, so the numbers must survive someone checking them.

## Deliverables

1. **The $10 era, dated.** What years could a typical short city ride
   (concert home, airport to hotel) actually cost ~$10, and what stage was
   Uber at (pre-IPO, VC-subsidized, growth-over-profit)? A date range plus
   one sentence of company context.
2. **Same trip, today's price.** A current Uber quote for one of the same
   trip shapes. Best form: a screenshot of the app's price for a concrete
   route (this can be Ben's own phone — primary source, no citation needed).
   Second best: published reporting with a comparable route price.
3. **The increase, quantified.** A citable figure for how much fares rose
   once the subsidy ended.

## Leads to verify, not conclusions

- Slate, "The Decade of Cheap Uber Rides Is Over" (May 2022): average prices
  rose 92% between 2018 and 2021 per Rakuten data; a separate analysis says
  45% between 2019 and 2022.
- Mid-2010s reporting that rider fares covered roughly 40% of ride cost
  (VC subsidized the rest) — find the primary source if it exists.
- LA Times (May 2019), "Uber fares are cheap, thanks to venture capital."

## Output

- Facts with their sources listed per claim, so Ben can read each source
  before it is cited (deck rule: no source enters the deck unread by Ben).
- Screenshot assets, if any, go to
  `public/images/blog/open-weights-ai-models/screenshots/` — they are used
  as-is, not through the background-removal pipeline.
- Write findings as a short section appended to this file.
