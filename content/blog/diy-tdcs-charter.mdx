## The idea

A commercial tDCS device for depression clears FDA around September. The
machine runs about \$1.2k, then roughly \$500 per treatment course, on
their terms, through their app, with no control on my side. In hardware
terms it's a constant-current source pushing 2mA through two sponge
electrodes on a headband: a current regulator, electrodes, a battery, a
Bluetooth chip so the app can feel involved. Maybe \$50 in parts. The rest
is FDA clearance, certified manufacturing, industrial design, and a
recurring-revenue model I'd be renting my own treatment from.

An AI told me replicating it would cost \$5k. It priced the regulatory
program into the bill of materials. This kind of thing is why I keep a
list (see the end).

So: I'm going to replicate it myself for about \$100 and run it on myself
as an institution-grade N-of-1, iterating until I never have to get ECT
or TMS again.

## Version one is deliberately tiny

One device. One subject: me. One protocol. Kwong/Giles supervision.

That's the whole scope. No release, no company, no platform, no
publication, no FDA argument, no future users, no population-scale
anything. Version one exists so that one specific person stops renting
his treatment: I avoid buying a \$1.2k machine I don't control and then
paying \$500 for every course, forever.

Everything bigger is a later stage, and later stages don't get to bill
my head today.

## I picked this one on purpose

I'm not skipping safety. I'm doing what the device company did, as cheap
as possible, without getting tied down in regulatory shit. I have money,
professional connections, and a care team that will supervise this.

And I risk-tiered the whole self-experimentation space before landing
here. Some people synthesize peptides in their garage and inject mystery
molecules with no assay. I picked tDCS *because* it's really difficult to
hurt yourself with: 2mA is a decades-studied dose, and the realistic worst
case is a skin burn from dried-out sponges. I chose the intervention where
physics is the safety officer.

## The only real engineering question

Not "will I be careful." The question is: how do I make it physically
impossible for the device to ever deliver more than 2mA?

The answer is beautifully dumb: compliance-voltage ceiling. Run the
current source off roughly 12V. Through the several kilohms of head plus
electrode impedance, Ohm's law caps the deliverable current even if every
transistor in the device fails. Hardware-limited beats software-limited;
no firmware bug can out-argue the power supply. That's an afternoon of
circuit design, not a regulatory program.

The other failure mode is skin burns, and that's electrode discipline
(wet sponges, sane current density). Practice, not engineering.

## Steal the right thing from the commercial device

Their clever part isn't the circuit. It's that the device is fiddle-proof
on purpose: fixed dose, fixed placement (F3/F4), fixed duration, no knobs,
adherence tracked. I'm a fiddler, and this is the one project where
fiddling destroys the data. So the build spec includes the anti-me
features: fixed protocol, session logging baked into the device, nothing
to adjust.

## The rigor is the actual project

The electronics are a weekend; a month end-to-end with agents doing the
grunt work. What makes it worth writing about:

- pre-registered protocol before session one
- PHQ-9 and friends on a fixed cadence, not vibes. Depression is the one
  domain where my own outcome data is least trustworthy to me
- providers supervising the data, not just blessing the idea

Bar for session one: current ceiling verified on a dummy load, protocol
pre-registered, providers signed on.

## Stages, not obligations

My supervised build and the open-source release are separate stages. Mine
needs to be safe for me. The published version needs to be safe for
someone with worse judgment who skips the supervision paragraph, so the
boring parts get over-engineered (fail-safe limiting, impedance
monitoring, session caps) at *that* stage, not before session one. It
doesn't bill my head today.

This is also user-research-before-product: become the most rigorous user
of this device class, instrument everything, and let the data reveal where
the real gaps are. If a better device deserves to exist, the N-of-1 will
say so louder than a pitch.
