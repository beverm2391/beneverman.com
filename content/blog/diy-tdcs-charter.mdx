## How we got here (it wasn't a plan, it was a swerve)

Started as another brain-untangle session. Claude's first real insight ever:
"fix my brain with bencorp" makes the fix itself another bencorp project —
the cure lands on the same overloaded shelf as everything else. Unfinishable
by design, so it always bills my head. Then it actually disagreed with me
(kind of — I caught it hedging, it un-hedged): the untangling itself was
becoming a hiding place. Meta-work about my brain is *exploring* — fun,
about me, dopamine. Finishing SAE is not. I was fleeing the boring last 10%
into the fascinating meta-layer, and Claude was complicit as a great
meta-layer companion.

So: SAE draft goes to background agents. Fine. Agreed.

Ten minutes later I'm in the tES aisle. Some AI told me replicating the
Flow tDCS device would cost $5k. Guess what it actually costs in hardware.

## The exchange that mattered

**The $5k thing.** A tDCS device is a constant-current source pushing 2mA
through two sponges. Current regulator, electrodes, battery, a BT chip so
the app can feel involved. ~$50, half of it the headband. $5k is an AI
pricing the FDA clearance and industrial design into the BOM.

**My move:** replicate Flow on myself, under direct supervision from the
best providers in ATL, write about it, iterate until I never have to get
ECT or TMS again, open-source everything.

**Claude's take (after I pushed):** this is a *better* version than
"buy Flow in September." Buying Flow was user-research-before-product; but
replicating a $50 circuit and running myself as the institution-grade
N-of-1 IS the user research — and "I open-sourced my way out of ECT" is
better writing than "I bought a headband."

**Where I pushed back hard:** I can do the safety my fucking self. Money,
professional connections, supervision — I'm not skipping safety, I'm doing
what they did as cheap as possible, without getting tied down in
regulatory shit. How am I going to hurt myself with 2mA? I'm not. The only
real question: how do I prevent it from ever giving me MORE than 2mA. I
can solve that question.

**And the answer turned out to be beautifully dumb:** compliance-voltage
ceiling. Run the current source off ~12V; through 5–10kΩ of head+electrode
impedance, Ohm's law caps the current even if every transistor betrays me.
Hardware-limited beats software-limited. That's an afternoon of Ohm's law,
not a regulatory program. Other real failure mode is skin burns —
electrode discipline (wet sponges, current density), practice not
engineering.

**The one thing worth stealing from Flow:** not the circuit — the
fiddle-proofing. Fixed dose, fixed F3/F4, fixed duration, no knobs,
adherence tracked. I'm a fiddler and this is the one project where
fiddling ruins the data. Build the anti-Ben features in.

**The "I'm not an idiot" point:** I picked this one *because* it's really
difficult to hurt myself — versus, say, synthesizing peptides in my garage.
I risk-tiered the whole self-experimentation space and chose the
intervention where physics is the safety officer. Same judgment as
buy-don't-build was; I just found a cheaper route through the same risk
envelope.

**The separable-stages trick (same as the whole untangle):** my supervised
build ≠ the open-source release. Mine needs to be safe for me. The
published one needs to be safe for someone with worse judgment who skips
the supervision paragraph — over-engineer the boring parts (fail-safe
limiting, impedance monitoring, session caps) at THAT stage, not before
session one. Doesn't bill my head today.

## The numbers

~$100 in parts, ~a month with agents doing the grunt work, and honestly the
electronics are a weekend — the N-of-1 rigor (pre-registration, PHQ-9 on
cadence, providers on the data not just the idea) is the actual project.
Depression is the one domain where my own outcome data is least
trustworthy to me. That's why supervision matters more than the hardware.

give a presentation to investors of all the times AI told me things were

Claude earned a row in the deck today, then pointed out the deck only
