## The idea in one breath

A commercial tDCS device for depression clears FDA ~September. Electrically
it's a constant-current source, two sponge electrodes, a headband — maybe
$50 of parts under $500 of clearance and industrial design. Plan: replicate
it for ~$100, run it on myself under direct supervision from my providers
as an institution-grade N-of-1, iterate until ECT and TMS are permanently
off my escalation path, open-source everything.

## Why this one (risk tiering)

I picked tDCS *because* it's the hardest serious self-experiment to hurt
yourself with — not despite that. Contrast: garage peptide synthesis is
"mystery molecule, no assay, inject it." tDCS at 2mA is a decades-studied
dose where the realistic worst case is a skin burn from dried-out sponges.
The choice of experiment *is* the safety judgment.

## The entire safety case, in one circuit decision

The real question isn't "will I be careful," it's "how do I make overdose
physically impossible." Answer: low compliance-voltage ceiling. Run the
current source off ~12V; through several kΩ of head + electrode impedance,
Ohm's law caps deliverable current even if every transistor fails. Hardware
limit beats software limit — no firmware bug can exceed the power supply.
That's an afternoon of design, not a regulatory program.

Second failure mode: skin burns → electrode discipline (wet sponges, sane
current density). That's practice, not engineering.

## Steal Flow's actual clever part

Their moat isn't the circuit — it's that the device is **fiddle-proof on
purpose**. Fixed dose, fixed F3/F4 placement, fixed duration, no knobs,
adherence tracked. I'm a fiddler, and this is the one project where
fiddling destroys the data. So the build spec includes the anti-Ben
features: fixed protocol, session logging baked in, nothing to adjust.

## The rigor is the project

The electronics are a weekend. The N-of-1 is the work:

- pre-registered protocol before session one
- standardized measures (PHQ-9 etc.) on a fixed cadence, not vibes —
  depression is the domain where your own outcome data is least
  trustworthy to yourself
- providers supervising the data, not just blessing the idea

Bar for session one: current ceiling verified on a dummy load, protocol
pre-registered, providers signed on.

## Staging (so it doesn't all bill my head at once)

My build and the open-source release are **separable stages**. Mine needs
to be safe for me, supervised. A published build needs to be safe for
someone with worse judgment who skips the supervision paragraph — that
means over-engineering the boring parts (fail-safe limiting, impedance
monitoring, session caps) *before* files go up, and not one second before
that stage arrives.

## Where this sits in the arc

Not a tangent. The long arc is personal tooling → publish → real medical
research → eventually a lab. The device-world strategy is
user-research-before-product: be the most rigorous user of this device
class, instrument everything, and let the data reveal the real product
gaps. If a better device should exist, the N-of-1 will say so louder than
a pitch.
