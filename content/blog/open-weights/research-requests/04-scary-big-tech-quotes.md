# Research 04, really scary big tech quotes

The deck's evidence rule: the most visceral defensible fact, quoted exactly,
at length. This request hunts verbatim quotes from big tech executives and
internal documents that make the room cringe — words from their own mouths
about surveillance, addiction-by-design, control, and extraction. Every quote
must be verbatim with a primary or contemporaneous source Ben can read.

## What qualifies

- Executives or internal documents, in their own words. No journalist
  paraphrase presented as quotation.
- Themes that serve the deck: they know the harm and ship anyway; the user
  is the product; engagement over wellbeing; data kept, produced, or used
  against users; power over what users may know or say.
- Include the context sentence needed to use the quote honestly, and any
  authenticity caveats (disputed attribution, partial leak, settled
  litigation).

## Candidates to verify (not conclusions)

- The Bosworth "The Ugly" internal Facebook memo (2016) — the connect-people
  passage including the maybe-someone-dies line, verbatim, with Meta's
  response.
- Zuckerberg's 2004 IMs ("trust me" exchange) — verify authenticity status
  and exact text as published.
- Eric Schmidt: the "creepy line" remark and the "if you have something you
  don't want anyone to know" remark — exact wording, original interviews.
- Sean Parker (2017) on Facebook: "exploiting a vulnerability in human
  psychology" — exact transcript.
- Chamath Palihapitiya (2017): "short-term, dopamine-driven feedback loops"
  — exact transcript.
- Facebook Papers internal research lines on Instagram and teen girls —
  exact internal wording as published in filings.
- Anything comparably direct from OpenAI, Google, Amazon, TikTok, or Uber
  executives about users as data, retention mechanics, or pricing power.

## Output

- One block per quote: verbatim text, speaker, date, primary link, context,
  caveats. Rank by cringe-per-word. Append findings to this file; Ben reads
  before anything is cited.
