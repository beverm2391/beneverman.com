I’m building toward a technology company and research organization that uses AI and computational biology to make biological discovery faster, more legible, and more useful.

The near-term work is about tools and research systems: better ways to learn from genomic and multiomic data, generate and test hypotheses, and move from messy evidence to experiments worth running. Mental health is a lasting area of interest, alongside the broader question of how computation can help us understand complex biology and design better therapeutics.

This is the long arc, not a ten-year roadmap. The path should be shaped by evidence, by the problems that prove tractable, and by collaborators who bring capabilities I do not have. Over time, I hope the work can grow from software and research into a team, durable scientific programs, and—where the evidence earns it—clinical translation.

The constant is the direction: build technology that shortens the distance between a biological question and a meaningful answer.
